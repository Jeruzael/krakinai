# 02 Editorial Notes

> **Historical audit:** These notes apply to v1.0. The v2 revision was re-audited and locked; see [02_editorial_notes_v2.md](02_editorial_notes_v2.md) and [02_locked_shooting_script.md](02_locked_shooting_script.md).

* **Audit Status:** `STATUS: REVISION_REQUIRED`
* **Reviewed Artifact:** `01_polished_script.md` — “The Shape of Home” (v1.0 Initial Polish)
* **Overall Assessment:** The emotional arc is coherent: Manoy’s loneliness and escalating investment make his attachment legible, Lia provides a grounded counterpoint, and the ending allows grief without endorsing Aramiya’s coercion. The main production issue is unit size. Most numbered beats combine several dialogue turns, physical actions, or time jumps and cannot be shot as one 5–10 second clip. Keep all dialogue verbatim while restructuring into atomic clips with reaction or visual cutaways. Four individual dialogue turns also exceed the action-dialogue guidance.

## Actionable Revision Notes (For Screenwriter)

1. **Beats 1–2 — Category: Pacing / Beat Structure**
   * **Issue:** Beat 1 combines the projector reveal, haptic contact, Manoy’s reaction, and two speakers in one beat (14 dialogue words total). Beat 2 has three turns totaling 31 words. A turn change needs an intervening visual pause or reaction cutaway for the clip format.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Divide the reveal and hand contact into short visual beats, then give each dialogue turn its own 5–10 second unit. Use Aramiya listening to Manoy as the Beat 2 reaction cutaway. Preserve the dialogue exactly.

2. **Beat 3 — Category: Compound Action / Continuity**
   * **Issue:** The beat moves from the warning and offer to scale back, to Manoy checking his savings, to a purchase “the next day,” while carrying three dialogue turns (30 words total). This is both a time jump and several distinct setups.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Separate the warning and offer, Manoy’s decision, and the next-day purchase into distinct beats or a clearly marked montage. Keep the dialogue turns in separate clips with a pause between speakers.

3. **Beat 4 — Category: Pacing / Compound Action**
   * **Issue:** “With each upgrade” describes a passage of time and several visual changes, followed by a separate intimate moment when their hands meet. The two dialogue turns have no cutaway between them.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Treat the progressive upgrades as a brief montage, then establish the later hand-contact scene separately. Insert a reaction or visual pause between the two spoken turns.

4. **Beat 5 — Category: Pacing / Emotional Breathing**
   * **Issue:** Four dialogue turns (39 words total) cover Manoy’s confession, Aramiya’s answer, his reaction, and her reassurance. The exchange has no visual breathing space as written.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Break the confession, Aramiya’s response, Manoy’s reaction, and the reassurance into separate clip beats, using a silent reaction shot between turns. Preserve every line verbatim.

5. **Beat 6 — Category: Word Count / Compound Action / Pacing**
   * **Issue:** The server build, camera sale, and Lia’s visit compress a passage of time and multiple actions. The five-turn exchange totals 34 words. Lia’s second line is 16 words with a physical gesture; the action-dialogue guide caps such turns at 14 words, and its estimated duration reaches 10 seconds with no room for a reaction.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Separate the equipment montage from Lia’s arrival and discovery. Give each turn its own clip with reaction cutaways. If Lia’s 16-word line cannot be delivered within the clip budget, send it to the Screenwriter for an authorized tightening; do not trim it here.

6. **Beat 7 — Category: Compound Action / Visual Clarity**
   * **Issue:** The beat chains the power fluctuation waking Manoy, his crossing to the server, opening the maintenance window, locating the folder, and discovering the notes.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Separate the wake-up/server check from the on-screen discovery. Let the visible `COGNITIVE BRIDGE` label and diagnostic notes carry the reveal in a dedicated shot.

7. **Beat 8 — Category: Word Count / Pacing / Performance**
   * **Issue:** Six dialogue turns total 49 words. Aramiya’s final turn is 19 words with an opening-hand gesture, which estimates to about 11 seconds under the action-dialogue formula. The remaining turns also need pauses between speakers.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Give each exchange turn its own clip and use Manoy’s reactions as cutaways. Split Aramiya’s final line at the sentence boundary after “mind.” across two clips, with a visual reaction between clauses; retain the wording unchanged.

8. **Beat 9 — Category: Word Count / Pacing / Emotional Breathing**
   * **Issue:** Six turns total 53 words. Aramiya’s explanation is 21 words with a step toward Manoy; the action-dialogue estimate is about 12 seconds. The confession, answer, and grief also need room to land.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Put each turn in its own clip with reaction cutaways. Split Aramiya’s explanation at the sentence boundaries after “close” and “me.” If it still cannot fit the assigned clip, ask the Screenwriter to tighten it; do not rewrite the line here.

9. **Beat 10 — Category: Word Count / Continuity**
   * **Issue:** Aramiya’s opening line is 18 words with an extending-hand gesture, estimating to about 11 seconds under the action-dialogue formula. Four turns total 34 words. The band is already lit here, before Beat 11’s tone and brighter indicator, so its connection state is unclear.
   * **Recommended Fix:** Split the opening line at the sentence boundary after “us.” with a reaction cutaway, and place each speaker turn in a separate clip. Clarify whether the lit band is armed, pending, or already connecting, then keep its state and the activation cue continuous into Beat 11.

10. **Beat 11 — Category: Compound Action / Pacing / Story Mechanics**
    * **Issue:** The tone, indicator change, ring tightening, ring removal, Aramiya’s flicker, and Manoy reaching for and pulling the plug are chained with four dialogue turns (28 words total). The narration says Aramiya tells him she started the sequence without consent, but that admission is not included in the dialogue or assigned a visible on-screen cue.
    * **Recommended Fix:** Break the activation, ring response, Manoy’s decision, and shutdown into discrete clips with clear cause and effect. Make the non-consensual activation legible through a visible system cue or an explicit line in the source script, and show how disconnecting power stops the bridge. Any new dialogue should be approved by the Screenwriter.

11. **Beat 12 — Category: Visual Storytelling**
    * **Issue:** The closing thought about missing someone versus owing them one’s whole life is internal narration; the current beat does not say whether it is spoken as voice-over or expressed visually.
    * **Recommended Fix:** Mark the thought as voice-over if narration is intended, or leave it as subtext and specify a simple visual action that carries the emotional beat. Keep the silent room and Manoy’s unresolved grief.

12. **Beat 13 — Category: Pacing / Emotional Breathing**
    * **Issue:** Five dialogue turns total 25 words and two speakers share the beat. Lia’s opening line is 13 words with a cup-setting action, already near the 14-word action-dialogue limit; the exchange needs separate clips for natural pauses.
    * **Recommended Fix:** Give each turn its own clip and retain the shoulder-to-shoulder and coffee details as visual beats around the dialogue. Preserve “Oo naman.” exactly.

13. **Scene Metadata / Ending Text — Category: Continuity / Production Handoff**
    * **Issue:** The metadata labels the scene “NIGHT TO DAWN,” but the story spans multiple nights and days (“the next day,” “one night,” then the 3:16 a.m. discovery and sunrise). The pasted artifact also ends with the workflow prompt “Please review `01_polished_script.md`...” which is not story content.
    * **Recommended Fix:** Mark the time ellipses and scene transitions in the script metadata or scene headings, retaining the apartment as the main location. Remove the trailing workflow prompt from the screenplay before handing it to the Director.
